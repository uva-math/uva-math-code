General Exam - Algebra

August 14, 2000

  1. Let VV be a vector space over the field of reals ℝ\mathbb{R}. Show that VV cannot be presented as the union of three proper subspaces.

  2. Let KK be an arbitrary field, GG be a finite group of order n>1n>1. Show that the group algebra K[G]K[G] has zero divisors. Does this result remain true for infinite groups?

  3. Prove or disprove: A commutative local ring has only one nonzero prime ideal (by definition, a commutative ring is called local if it has only one maximal ideal).

  4. For a field KK, we denote by K(x)K(x) the field of rational functions in one variable xx over KK. Prove or disprove: ℂ(x)\mathbb{C}(x) is the algebraic closure of ℝ(x)\mathbb{R}(x).
    5 . Let KK be the splitting field of the polynomial f(x)=x13−1f(x)=x^{13}-1 over 𝐅5\mathbf{F}_{5} (the field of 5 elements). Determine the Galois group Gal⁡(K/𝐅5)\operatorname{Gal}\left(K / \mathbf{F}_{5}\right).

  5. Let aa and bb be positive relatively prime integers. Show that any integer N>abN>a b can be written in the form N=ax+byN=a x+b y where x,yx, y are integers ≥0\geq 0.

  6. (a) Let GG be a finite group of order pαmp^{\alpha} m where pp is a prime relatively prime to mm, and let GpG_{p} denote the set of elements of GG whose order is pip^{i} for some i>0i>0. Show that

|Gp|≤[G:N](pα−1)\left|G_{p}\right| \leq[G: N]\left(p^{\alpha}-1\right)

where NN is the normalizer of some Sylow pp-subgroup, with equality holding iff any two distinct Sylow pp-subgroups have trivial intersection.
(b) Let GG be a finite group of order pαqβp^{\alpha} q^{\beta} for distinct primes p,qp, q. Assume that GG does not have elements of mixed order (i.e. the order of any element is either pip^{i} or qjq^{j} ) and has a normal Sylow qq-subgroup. Show that

|Gp|=qβ(pα−1)\left|G_{p}\right|=q^{\beta}\left(p^{\alpha}-1\right)

Using (a), conclude that in this case any two distinct Sylow pp-subgroups intersect trivially.
8. Determine all possibilities for the Jordan canonical form of a matrix A∈GL2(ℂ)A \in G L_{2}(\mathbb{C}) given that AA is conjugate to tA−1{ }^{t} A^{-1} where t{ }^{t} denotes the operation of taking the transpose of a matrix.
9. Let RR denote the set of complex numbers of the form a+b−6a+b \sqrt{-6} with a,b∈ℤa, b \in \mathbb{Z}.
(i) Show that RR is a subring of ℂ\mathbb{C}.
(ii) Let 𝔞\mathfrak{a} be the ideal of RR generated by 5 and 2+−62+\sqrt{-6}. Show that 𝔞\mathfrak{a} is proper (i.e. 𝔞≠R\mathfrak{a} \neq R ). (Hint. Use the conjugate 2−−62-\sqrt{-6}.)
(iii) Show that the ideal 𝔞\mathfrak{a} is prime, while the ideal 𝔟⊂R\mathfrak{b} \subset R generated by 5 alone is not prime. (Hint. For the first part, try to identify the quotient ring R/𝔞R / \mathfrak{a}.)
10. Let RR be a unital commutative ring. From the usual theory of determinants for matrices over RR, we know that if M≃RnM \simeq R^{n} is a free module we can uniquely define the determinant det⁡(T)\operatorname{det}(T) to any T∈End⁡R(M)T \in \operatorname{End}_{R}(M). Suppose MM is only a direct summand of a free module, M⊕M′≃RnM \oplus M^{\prime} \simeq R^{n} for some (finite) nn. Define T̃=T⊕1M′∈End⁡R(M⊕M′)\tilde{T}=T \oplus 1_{M^{\prime}} \in \operatorname{End}_{R}\left(M \oplus M^{\prime}\right) and set

detM,M′(T):=det⁡(T̃)\operatorname{det}_{M, M^{\prime}}(T):=\operatorname{det}(\tilde{T})

Show that this is independent of the complement chosen: if also M⊕M′′≃RmM \oplus M^{\prime \prime} \simeq R^{m} and T̂=T⊕1M′′\hat{T}=T \oplus 1_{M^{\prime \prime}}, then det⁡(T̂)=det⁡(T̃)\operatorname{det}(\hat{T})=\operatorname{det}(\tilde{T}) as elements of RR. (Hint. Show that there are two

S1,S2∈End⁡R(M⊕M′⊕M⊕M′′)≃End⁡R(Rn+m)S_{1}, S_{2} \in \operatorname{End}_{R}\left(M \oplus M^{\prime} \oplus M \oplus M^{\prime \prime}\right) \simeq \operatorname{End}_{R}\left(R^{n+m}\right)

with det⁡(S1)=det⁡M,M′(T),det⁡(S2)=det⁡M,M′′(T)\operatorname{det}\left(S_{1}\right)=\operatorname{det}_{M, M^{\prime}}(T), \operatorname{det}\left(S_{2}\right)=\operatorname{det}_{M, M^{\prime \prime}}(T), and S2=PS1P−1S_{2}=P S_{1} P^{-1} for an invertible P∈End⁡R(M⊕M′⊕M⊕M′′)P \in \operatorname{End}_{R}\left(M \oplus M^{\prime} \oplus M \oplus M^{\prime \prime}\right).) Conclude that there is a welldefined determinant for endomorphisms of such MM ’s, which agrees with the usual determinant for free modules MM.
