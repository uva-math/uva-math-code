Topology General Exam — January 2026

Instructions: Please provide your ID number, do no sign your name!

This is a four hour exam. Your solutions should be legible and clearly organized, written in complete sentences in good mathematical style on your own paper. All work should be your own—no outside sources are permitted—using methods and results from the first year topology courses. Each problem is worth the same number of points.

Problem 1

Consider the 2-sphere S2S^2 and the 2-dimensional torus TT.
(a) For what values m∈ℤm \in \mathbb{Z} does there exist a degree mm map f:S2→Tf \colon S^2 \rightarrow T?
(b) For what values n∈ℤn \in \mathbb{Z} does there exist a degree nn map f:T→S2f \colon T \rightarrow S^2?
In both questions, if you state that a map of a certain degree exists then give a construction; otherwise prove its non-existence.

Problem 2

Let MM be a smooth manifold and let f:M→ℝf \colon M \rightarrow \mathbb{R} be a smooth function. Define a linear map Dfk:Ωk(M)→Ωk+1(M)D_f^k \colon \Omega^k(M) \rightarrow \Omega^{k+1}(M) by: Dfk(ω)=dω+df∧ωD_f^k(\omega) = d\omega + df \wedge \omega where dd is the standard exterior derivative.
(a) Show that Dfk+1∘Dfk=0D_f^{k+1} \circ D_f^k = 0.
(b) Let ω\omega be a kk-form. Show that Dfk(ω)=0D_f^k(\omega) = 0 if and only if the form efωe^f \omega is closed: d(efω)=0d(e^f \omega) = 0.
(c) Prove that the map ω↦efω\omega \mapsto e^f \omega defines a chain isomorphism between the chain complexes (Ω*(X),Df)(\Omega^*(X), D_f) and (Ω*(X),d)(\Omega^*(X), d).

Problem 3

Let F:ℝ3→ℝ3F \colon \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3 be the map defined by F(x,y,z)=(xz,yz,z)F(x, y, z) = (xz, yz, z). Let f:S2→ℝ3f \colon S^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3 be the restriction of FF to the unit sphere S2S^2. Determine the set of points p∈S2p \in S^2 at which ff fails to be an immersion.

Problem 4

Let GG be a Lie group. Show that its tangent bundle TGTG is trivial, that is isomorphic to G×ℝn→GG \times \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow G for some nn. (Recall that a Lie group GG is a smooth manifold which is also a group, such that the multiplication map (f,g)↦f⋅g(f, g) \mapsto f \cdot g, and the inverse map f↦f−1f \mapsto f^{-1} are smooth.)

Hint. Given g∈Gg \in G, the map Lg:G→GL_g \colon G \longrightarrow G, given by Lg(h)=ghL_g(h) = gh, may be helpful; note that it is a diffeomorphism.

Problem 5

Let XX be a 2-dimensional disk with nn distinct points on its boundary identified, n≥2n \geq 2. Let AA be its boundary ⋁i=1nS1\bigvee_{i=1}^{n} S^1.
(a) Compute π1(X)\pi_1(X) and π1(A)\pi_1(A).
(b) Show that there is no retraction r:X→Ar \colon X \rightarrow A.

Problem 6

Let X=(S1∨S1)×(S1∨S1)X = (S^1 \vee S^1) \times (S^1 \vee S^1).
(a) Compute π1(X)\pi_1(X).
(b) Compute the homology groups of XX.
(c) Show that every continuous map ℝP2→X\mathbb{R}P^2 \rightarrow X is nullhomotopic.

Problem 7

For all n≥0n \geq 0, compute the homology groups with ℤ/2\mathbb{Z}/2-coefficients of the space XX obtained from the standard nn-simplex Δn\Delta^n by identifying all faces of the same dimension.
