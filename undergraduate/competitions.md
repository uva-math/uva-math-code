<h1 class="mb-3">Competitions</h1>

Mathematics competitions are open to all UVA undergraduates, not only to math majors, and no prior competition experience is expected.

---

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">Putnam Competition</h2>

The [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://maa.org/putnam/) is the main undergraduate mathematics competition in the United States and Canada. The **87th Putnam will be held on Saturday, December 5, 2026**, and will be proctored on Grounds; the room and the schedule for the day will be announced here and by the Math Club in the fall.

Beginning with the 2026 exam, the format changes from two three-hour sessions to **four 90-minute sessions of three problems each** — twelve problems in total, worth 10 points apiece, with breaks between sessions. The competition is open to undergraduates who have not yet received a college degree, and each student may take it at most four times.

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">Mathematical Contest in Modeling (MCM/ICM)</h2>

The [Mathematical Contest in Modeling and the Interdisciplinary Contest in Modeling](https://www.comap.com/contests/mcm-icm), run by COMAP, are 96-hour team competitions in applied and computational mathematics. Teams of up to three undergraduates choose one open-ended real-world problem and submit a written solution paper — there is no proctored exam, and programming and writing count as much as the mathematics.

The **2027 contest runs from 5:00 p.m. EST on Thursday, January 28, 2027, to 8:00 p.m. EST on Monday, February 1, 2027**. Teams must be registered by a faculty advisor before 3:00 p.m. EST on January 28, and the registration fee is $100 per team. Students interested in forming a team should contact the [Director of Undergraduate Studies]({{site.url}}/undergrad/contacts/) about finding an advisor.

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">AWM Student Essay Contest</h2>

The [Association for Women in Mathematics Student Essay Contest](https://awm-math.org/awards/student-essay-contest/) asks for a 500–1000 word essay based on an interview with a woman, or a person of an underrepresented gender identity, working in or retired from a career in the mathematical sciences. Submissions open on November 1 and close on February 1, with a separate category for undergraduates; winning essays are published by AWM.

The [AWM Student Chapter at UVA]({{site.url}}/awm/) can help with finding an interview subject.

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">Undergraduate Statistics Project Competition (USPROC)</h2>

CAUSE and the American Statistical Association run two annual competitions for work students have already done in statistics and data science, so no separate contest sitting is involved. [USCLAP](https://www.causeweb.org/usproc/usclap) is for class projects and takes a report of up to three pages; [USRESP](https://www.causeweb.org/usproc/usresp) is for research projects — REU work, senior projects, honors theses, capstones — and takes a paper of up to twenty pages. Students may work alone or in groups.

There are two submission cycles each year: one in December, for summer and fall projects, and one in June, for winter and spring projects and for year-long work. Current dates are posted at the [USPROC site](https://www.causeweb.org/usproc/).

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">AWM Alice T. Schafer Prize</h2>

The [Alice T. Schafer Prize](https://awm-math.org/awards/schafer-prize-for-undergraduates/) is awarded annually by the Association for Women in Mathematics for excellence in mathematics by an undergraduate woman. **Nominations are accepted from August 1 to September 15** each year through MathPrograms.org. Anyone may nominate, and students may nominate themselves, in which case one additional letter of support is required.

<h2 class="bodoni-h2">Presenting undergraduate research</h2>

The [Joint Mathematics Meetings](https://jointmathematicsmeetings.org/jmm), the largest annual mathematics meeting in the country, will be held **January 12–15, 2027, at McCormick Place in Chicago**. It includes a Pi Mu Epsilon poster session for undergraduates presenting original research, with awards for outstanding presentations; purely expository posters are not accepted.

**Abstracts for the Pi Mu Epsilon poster session are due September 29, 2026**, with notification in October. The deadline for all other JMM abstracts is September 15, 2026.
