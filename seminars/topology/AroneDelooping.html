Delooping the Connecting Maps in the Taylor Tower

Greg Arone — January 18, 2007. Joint work with W. Dwyer and K. Lesh.

Transcribed from three-page handwritten notes. Download the printable transcript (PDF). Arrow diagrams are also described in words. Abbreviated notation in the notes is retained.

Page 1: Taylor towers and connecting maps

Given XX, get a tower of fibrations. This gives a way to pass from stable to unstable information. The Taylor tower of the identity has compatible maps from XX to every stage: ⋯→P3(X)→P2(X)→QX=Ω∞Σ∞X.\cdots\longrightarrow P_3(X)\longrightarrow P_2(X)\longrightarrow QX=\Omega^\infty\Sigma^\infty X. For good spaces, limnPn(X)=X.\lim_nP_n(X)=X.

We know a lot about this: Dn(X)=Fiber⁡(Pn(X)→Pn−1(X))≃Ω∞((Wn∧X∧n)hΣn).D_n(X)=\operatorname{Fiber}(P_n(X)\longrightarrow P_{n-1}(X)) \simeq\Omega^\infty\bigl((W_n\wedge X^{\wedge n})_{h\Sigma_n}\bigr). The annotations identify WnW_n as the derivative, X∧nX^{\wedge n} with the xnx^n term, and homotopy orbits with the 1/n!1/n! term of a Taylor series.

Since these are infinite loop spaces, there are maps Dn(X)→Pn(X)→Pn−1(X)→BDn(X).D_n(X)\longrightarrow P_n(X)\longrightarrow P_{n-1}(X)\longrightarrow BD_n(X). Thus there are connecting maps, in the style of kk-invariants, Dn−1(X)→BDn(X).D_{n-1}(X)\longrightarrow BD_n(X). These give the d1d_1 differential in the spectral sequence for π*(X)\pi_*(X) using the tower.

Fix a prime pp and let X=SmX=S^m: mm odd when p>2p>2, and any mm when p=2p=2.

Theorem. In this case Dn(X)≃*D_n(X)\simeq * unless n=pkn=p^k.

We can then regrade the tower. Put DIk=Dpk(X)DI_k=D_{p^k}(X). The diagram is ⋯→Pp2(X)→Pp(X)→P1(X)=QX,\cdots\longrightarrow P_{p^2}(X)\longrightarrow P_p(X)\longrightarrow P_1(X)=QX, with fiber maps DI2→Pp2(X)DI_2\to P_{p^2}(X) and DI1→Pp(X)DI_1\to P_p(X).

For the connecting map, use the equivalence Ppk−1(X)≃Ppk−1(X)P_{p^k-1}(X)\simeq P_{p^{k-1}}(X) and the map from DIk−1DI_{k-1} into the latter stage. Composing with the map to BDIkBDI_k gives the connecting map labeled αk−1\alpha_{k-1}: αk−1:DIk−1→BDIk.\alpha_{k-1}:DI_{k-1}\longrightarrow BDI_k.

There is another functor, from complex inner-product vector spaces to spaces, V↦BU(V).V\longmapsto BU(V). There is a Weiss calculus starting with BU=lim→VBU(V).BU=\underset{V}{\underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}}BU(V). After pp-completing, there is again a faster collapse.

Page 2: Deloopings and filtrations

The tower for BU(V)BU(V) has compatible maps to ⋯→Tp(V)→Tp0(V)→T0(V)=BU,\cdots\longrightarrow T_p(V)\longrightarrow T_{p^0}(V)\longrightarrow T_0(V)=BU, where T0T_0 is the constant term. The derivatives are written in the notes as DUk(V)≃Ω∞((W_pk∧SpkV)hU(pk)).DU_k(V)\simeq\Omega^\infty\bigl((\underline{W}_{p^k}\wedge S^{p^kV})_{hU(p^k)}\bigr). Here the first factor is annotated as a U(pk)U(p^k)-spectrum, and SpkVS^{p^kV} as the one-point compactification (ℂpk⊗V)+(\mathbb{C}^{p^k}\otimes V)^+.

Again there are connecting maps βk:DUk→BDUk+1.\beta_k:DU_k\longrightarrow BDU_{k+1}. These are not infinite loop maps, but:

Theorem. αk\alpha_k and βk\beta_k are kk-fold loop maps. Thus, for example, there exists α̃k:BkDIk→Bk+1DIk+1,αk≃Ωkα̃k.\widetilde\alpha_k:B^kDI_k\longrightarrow B^{k+1}DI_{k+1}, \qquad \alpha_k\simeq\Omega^k\widetilde\alpha_k.

Example: S1S^1. Even in this case, αk\alpha_k is not an infinite loop map. The diagram has S1→Pp(S1)S^1\to P_p(S^1) and S1→QS1S^1\to QS^1, the map Pp(S1)→QS1P_p(S^1)\to QS^1, the fiber map DI1→Pp(S1)DI_1\to P_p(S^1), and the connecting map QS1→BDI1QS^1\to BDI_1.

Using the deloopings gives a sequence of spaces S1→QS1→BDI1→B2DI2→⋯.S^1\longrightarrow QS^1\longrightarrow BDI_1\longrightarrow B^2DI_2\longrightarrow\cdots. The forward maps labeled αk\alpha_k are not infinite loop maps. The backward maps fkf_k, drawn as dashed arrows in the notes, are infinite loop maps coming from Sp⁡∞(X)≃K(H*X).\operatorname{Sp}^{\infty}(X)\simeq K(H_*X). The inclusions S0→Sp⁡2(S0)→⋯→Sp⁡∞(S0)=HℤS^0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}^{2}(S^0)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}^{\infty}(S^0)=H\mathbb{Z} give a filtration of HℤH\mathbb{Z}. Also, pp-locally, Sp⁡n(S0)/Sp⁡n−1(S0)≃*\operatorname{Sp}^n(S^0)/\operatorname{Sp}^{n-1}(S^0)\simeq * unless n=pkn=p^k. One also gets Hℤ←S0←Σ−1(Sp⁡p(S0)/Sp⁡1(S0))←⋯.H\mathbb{Z}\longleftarrow S^0\longleftarrow \Sigma^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Sp}^p(S^0)/\operatorname{Sp}^1(S^0)\bigr)\longleftarrow\cdots.

Looping the other sequence gives ℤ←QS0←ΩBDI1←⋯,\mathbb{Z}\longleftarrow QS^0\longleftarrow\Omega BDI_1\longleftarrow\cdots, which is Ω∞\Omega^\infty of the tower above. The term ΩBDI1\Omega BDI_1 is annotated QBΣpQB\Sigma_p.

There is an analogous filtration in the orthogonal case: bu→A1→⋯→Hℤ.bu\longrightarrow A_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow H\mathbb{Z}.

Page 3: The conjecture and a proof idea

The Weiss calculus gives BU(1)→BU→BDU0→B2DU1→⋯,BU(1)\longrightarrow BU\longrightarrow BDU_0\longrightarrow B^2DU_1\longrightarrow\cdots, where BU(1)=ℂℙ∞BU(1)=\mathbb{CP}^{\infty} and BDU0=QΣ2ℂℙ∞BDU_0=Q\Sigma^2\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

Taking Ω2\Omega^2 gives ℤ→ℤ×BU→Qℂℙ∞→Ω2B2DU1→⋯.\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\times BU\longrightarrow Q\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\longrightarrow\Omega^2B^2DU_1\longrightarrow\cdots. There are forward maps βk\beta_k and backward maps gkg_k. This is the same as the filtration from above.

Conjecture. There exist deloopings αk,βk\alpha_k,\beta_k that act as a contracting homotopy; for example, fk∘αk+αk+1∘fk+1≃Id⁡.f_k\circ\alpha_k+\alpha_{k+1}\circ f_{k+1}\simeq\operatorname{Id}. Consequently the “chain complexes” are “exact,” and the homotopy spectral sequence collapses at E2E_2.

Idea of proof. How can we show a map is a kk-fold loop map? Given ΩkX→ΩkY\Omega^kX\to\Omega^kY, find the image of Ωk:Map⁡(X,Y)→Map⁡(ΩkX,ΩkY).\Omega^k:\operatorname{Map}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(\Omega^kX,\Omega^kY). These are too big. Instead look at natural transformations. The notes write the comparison as Nat⁡(Bk(−),Bk+1(−))→ΩkNat⁡(DIk(−),BDIk+1(−)).\operatorname{Nat}(B^k(-),B^{k+1}(-)) \xrightarrow{\Omega^k} \operatorname{Nat}(DI_k(-),BDI_{k+1}(-)). In the Weiss story, only consider V↦S1∧SVV\mapsto S^1\wedge S^V.

The last line singles out maps of the form ΩdΩ∞Σ∞(X∧2k)h≀kΣ2→ΩdQ(X∧2k+1)h≀k+1Σ2.\Omega^d\Omega^\infty\Sigma^\infty \bigl(X^{\wedge 2^k}\bigr)_{h\,\wr^k\Sigma_2} \longrightarrow \Omega^dQ\bigl(X^{\wedge 2^{k+1}}\bigr)_{h\,\wr^{k+1}\Sigma_2}.
