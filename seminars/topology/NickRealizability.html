Some Topological Non-Realizability Results

Nick Kuhn — September 11, 2008.

Transcribed from three-page handwritten notes. Download the printable transcript (PDF). Diagram arrows and annotations are described in words as well as formulas.

Page 1: Realizing graded rings

Given a space XX, its cohomology H*(X)H^*(X) is a graded-commutative ring. That is, H*(−)H^*(-) is a functor from the homotopy category of spaces to graded-commutative rings, written in the notes as HoTop→GrComRing.\mathrm{HoTop}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GrComRing}.

Question. What graded rings can be topologically realized? Given R*R^*, is there a space XX with H*(X)=R*H^*(X)=R^*?

The most famous version is the Steenrod problem (1961): if H*(X)=ℤ[y2d1,…,y2dn],H^*(X)=\mathbb Z[y_{2d_1},\ldots,y_{2d_n}], what can d1,…,dnd_1,\ldots,d_n be?

Examples. H*(ℂℙ∞)=ℤ[x2],H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})=\mathbb Z[x_2], H*(BSU(n))=ℤ[x4,x6,…,x2n],x2i=ci,H^*(BSU(n))=\mathbb Z[x_4,x_6,\ldots,x_{2n}],\qquad x_{2i}=c_i, H*(BSp(n))=ℤ[x4,x8,…,x4n],x4i=pi.H^*(BSp(n))=\mathbb Z[x_4,x_8,\ldots,x_{4n}],\qquad x_{4i}=p_i.

Theorem (Andersen–Grodal, 2007). If H*(X)H^*(X) is polynomial, it is isomorphic to a product of these. This is closely related to the theory of finite loop spaces.

One can pose these questions with coefficients in a field 𝔽\mathbb F. In characteristic zero, H*(ΩS2n+1;ℚ)=ℚ[x2n],H^*(\Omega S^{2n+1};\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q[x_{2n}], so any polynomial algebra over ℚ\mathbb Q works.

Characteristic p>0p>0 is much more exciting: mod pp cohomology has extra structure as a module over 𝒜\mathcal A, the Steenrod algebra.

For p=2p=2, there are natural group homomorphisms Sq⁡k:H*(−)→H*+k(−).\operatorname{Sq}^k:H^*(-)\longrightarrow H^{*+k}(-). The algebra 𝒜\mathcal A is generated by Sq⁡k\operatorname{Sq}^k, for 0≤k<∞0\leq k<\infty, subject to the Adem relations. It has the same size as a polynomial algebra with generators in degrees 2k−12^k-1, for k≥1k\geq1.

Then H*(X;𝔽2)H^*(X;\mathbb F_2) is an 𝒜\mathcal A-module.

Page 2: Unstable modules and unstable algebras

The module satisfies the unstable condition: if x∈Hn(X;𝔽2)x\in H^n(X;\mathbb F_2), then Sq⁡ix=0for i>n.\operatorname{Sq}^ix=0\quad\text{for }i>n. Thus H*(X)H^*(X) belongs to 𝒰\mathcal U, the category of unstable 𝒜\mathcal A-modules. The diagram shows H*(−)H^*(-) taking values both in 𝒰\mathcal U and in graded-commutative algebras; these structures interact.

Question 2. What unstable 𝒜\mathcal A-modules can be realized?

  1. Cartan formula: Sq⁡k(x∪y)=∑i=0kSq⁡i(x)∪Sq⁡k−i(y).\operatorname{Sq}^k(x\cup y)=\sum_{i=0}^k\operatorname{Sq}^i(x)\cup\operatorname{Sq}^{k-i}(y). [Transcription note: the scan writes xx in the second factor on the right; that argument is yy in the Cartan formula.]
  2. Restriction axiom: if x∈Hk(X)x\in H^k(X), then Sq⁡k(x)=x∪x.\operatorname{Sq}^k(x)=x\cup x. A margin annotation compares this with a restricted Lie algebra. Steenrod called these the “reduced squares.”

Example. H*(ℝℙ∞;𝔽2)=𝔽2[t],t∈H1,H^*(\mathbb{RP}^{\infty};\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[t],\qquad t\in H^1, Sq⁡k(tn)=(nk)tn+k.\operatorname{Sq}^k(t^n)=\binom nk t^{n+k}.

An unstable algebra is an algebra which is an unstable 𝒜\mathcal A-module satisfying the Cartan formula and the restriction axiom. Let 𝒦\mathcal K be the category of unstable 𝒜\mathcal A-algebras. Then H*(−;𝔽2):HoTop→𝒦.H^*(-;\mathbb F_2):\mathrm{HoTop}\longrightarrow\mathcal K.

The 𝒜\mathcal A-module structure restricts the algebra structure.

Example. There does not exist XX such that H*(X)=𝔽2[x3]H^*(X)=\mathbb F_2[x_3]. Indeed, Sq⁡3x3=x32≠0,\operatorname{Sq}^3x_3=x_3^2\ne0, but Sq⁡3x3=Sq⁡1Sq⁡2x3=Sq⁡1(0)\operatorname{Sq}^3x_3=\operatorname{Sq}^1\operatorname{Sq}^2x_3 =\operatorname{Sq}^1(0) for degree reasons.

The algebra structure also restricts the module structure.

Example. Consider 𝒜⋅t⊆𝔽2[t]\mathcal A\cdot t\subseteq\mathbb F_2[t], denoted ϕ=⟨t,t2,t4,t8,…⟩.\phi=\langle t,t^2,t^4,t^8,\ldots\rangle. The diagram joins consecutive generators by Sq⁡1\operatorname{Sq}^1, Sq⁡2\operatorname{Sq}^2, Sq⁡4\operatorname{Sq}^4, and so on.

Is ϕ\phi the cohomology of a space? No. Writing its classes as x1,x2,x4,…x_1,x_2,x_4,\ldots, the relations Sq⁡1x1=x2,Sq⁡2x2=x4\operatorname{Sq}^1x_1=x_2,\qquad\operatorname{Sq}^2x_2=x_4 would imply x2=x12,x4=x22=x14,x_2=x_1^2,\qquad x_4=x_2^2=x_1^4, but there is no class x13x_1^3.

Page 3: Suspension and non-realizability

Conjecture. If MM is a finitely generated unstable 𝒜\mathcal A-module which is infinite-dimensional over 𝔽2\mathbb F_2, there does not exist XX with H̃*(X)≅M.\widetilde H^*(X)\cong M.

Theorem (Kuhn–Schwartz). This is true.

The suspension ΣX\Sigma X is drawn as two cones on XX glued along their common base. There is a suspension endofunctor of 𝒰\mathcal U, defined by (ΣM)n=Mn−1.(\Sigma M)^n=M^{n-1}. The notes record H*(ΣX)=ΣH*(X),H^*(\Sigma X)=\Sigma H^*(X), and that all cup products vanish.

Does there exist a space with H*(X)=ΣϕH^*(X)=\Sigma\phi? No.

  1. A hard proof uses the chain of operations Sq⁡1\operatorname{Sq}^1, Sq⁡2\operatorname{Sq}^2, Sq⁡4\operatorname{Sq}^4, and so on. The diagram singles out an arrow labeled Sq⁡16\operatorname{Sq}^{16}. A margin annotation says an additional cell would be needed there by the Hopf invariant one result.
  2. Lionel shows that a three-stage part cannot exist. The diagram for a putative space ZZ has classes x17x_{17}, x33x_{33}, and x65x_{65}, with successive arrows from the first to the second and from the second to the third. Try to compute H*(ΩZ)H^*(\Omega Z); enough can be obtained to get a contradiction.

Then replace ℤ/2\mathbb Z/2 by MM. The next diagram has three copies of MM, with arrows labeled Sq⁡2k\operatorname{Sq}^{2^k} and Sq⁡2k+1\operatorname{Sq}^{2^{k+1}}.

One needs many loops, depending on dim⁡M\dim M and other data. A recent paper allows an easier argument using the map written in the notes as Z→Σ∞ΩnZ.Z\longrightarrow\Sigma^\infty\Omega^nZ.
