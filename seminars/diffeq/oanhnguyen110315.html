Roots of Random Polynomials with Arbitrary Coefficients

Oanh Nguyen — November 3, 2015.

Download the printable abstract (PDF).

We consider the following model of random polynomials: Pn(x)=cnξnxn+⋯+c1ξ1x+c0ξ0x0,P_n(x)=c_n\xi_nx^n+\cdots+c_1\xi_1x+c_0\xi_0x^0, where the ξi\xi_i are independent random variables with bounded 2+ϵ2+\epsilon moments, and the cic_i are deterministic coefficients. One of the simplest examples is the Kac polynomial, in which ci=1c_i=1 for all ii. Classical theorems by Kac (1943), Wilkins (1988), and Edelman–Kostlan (1996) show that for the Kac polynomial with ξi\xi_i being independent identically distributed standard Gaussian variables, the expectation of the number of real roots of PnP_n is 2πlog⁡n+O(1).\frac2\pi\log n+O(1). It took considerable effort to prove the same asymptotics for the Kac polynomial with more general distributions of ξi\xi_i; for example, the works by Erdős–Offord (1956), Edelman–Kostan (1995), Nguyen–N–Vu (2015), and Do–Nguyen–Vu (2015). What about other non-Kac polynomials?

In this talk, we discuss optimal local universality for roots of PnP_n when the cic_i have polynomial growth and, as an application, we derive sharp estimates for the number of real roots of this polynomial. Our results also hold for series; in particular, we prove local universality for random hyperbolic series. This is joint work with Yen Do and Van Vu.
