Well-posedness for the Navier-Stokes equations

Herbert Koch
Institut für Angewandte Mathematik, Universität Heidelberg

Abstract

We study the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on ℝn×ℝ+\mathbb{R}^n\times\mathbb{R}^+

ut+(u⋅∇)u−Δu+∇p=0∇⋅u=0(1)u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p=0\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0\qquad(1)

where uu is the velocity and pp is the pressure, with initial data u(x,0)=u0(x)u(x,0)=u_0(x).

Existence of weak solutions has been shown by Leray. Uniqueness (and regularity) of weak solutions is unknown and both are among the major open questions in applied analysis. Under stronger assumptions there exist local and/or global smooth solutions. One version of this has been shown by Kato for initial data in Ln(ℝn)L^n(\mathbb{R}^n).

What are reasonable requirements for solutions and initial data?

I. The nonlinearity is quadratic. To have the notion of a weak solution we have to require for all xx and RR

u∈L2(BR(x)×[0,R2])(2)u\in L^2(B_R(x)\times[0,R^2])\qquad(2)

  1. The Navier-Stokes equations are invariant with respect to scaling. If uu is a solution (we omit the pressure in the notation, as well as a discussion what we mean by a solution), then

    ũ=λu(λx,λ2t)\tilde u=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)

    is again a solution. We require the scale invariant version of the L2L^2 condition above

    supx,t≤Tt−n/2∫Bt(x)∫0t|u|2dyds<∞.(3)\sup_{x,t\le T}t^{-n/2}\int_{B_{\sqrt t}(x)}\int_0^t|u|^2\,dy\,ds<\infty.\qquad(3)

  2. The linear term should be dominant. Let

    ∥v∥BMOT−1:=supx,t≤T(t−n/2∫Bt(x)∫0t|u|2dxds)1/2(4)\|v\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_T}:=\sup_{x,t\le T}\left(t^{-n/2}\int_{B_{\sqrt t}(x)}\int_0^t|u|^2\,dx\,ds\right)^{1/2}\qquad(4)

    where uu is the unique function which satisfies

    ut−Δu=0,u(x,0)=v(x).u_t-\Delta u=0,\qquad u(x,0)=v(x).

    We define the function space BMOT−1\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_T as the set of all tempered distributions for which the norm is finite.

Theorem 1. There exists δ>0\delta>0 with the following property: Let 0<T≤∞0<T\le\infty. If ∇⋅u0=0\nabla\cdot u_0=0 and ∥u0∥(BMOT−1)n≤δ\|u_0\|_{(\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_T)^n}\le\delta then there exists a unique smooth solution up to time TT.

The theorem implies for example existence of a unique local solution for initial velocity in LnL^n or L∞L^\infty.

The talk is based on joint work with D. Tataru.
