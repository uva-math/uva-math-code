Combination Theorems for Convergence Groups

  • Advisor: Sara Maloni
  • Defense date: April 8, 10 am
  • Location: Kerchof 111
  • Website: Alec Traaseth

Abstract

First studied by Poincaré and Klein in 1883, Kleinian groups are discrete subgroups of PSL⁡(2,ℂ)\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C), the orientation preserving isometries of the real 3-dimensional hyperbolic space, ℍℝ3\mathbb H^3_{\mathbb R}. These groups have deep connections to 3-manifolds, and Teichmuüller theory. In particular, geometrically finite Kleinian groups have been of great interest. A Kleinian group GG being geometrically finite roughly means the quotient ℍℝ3/G\mathbb H^3_{\mathbb R}/G decomposes into a compact piece containing all of the topology and finitely many ‘ends.’

The classical combination theorems of Maskit give sufficient combinatorial conditions stating when two geometrically finite Kleinian groups G1G_1 and G2G_2 generate a new geometrically finite Kleinian group GG isomorphic to either an HNN extension or amalgamated free product of the original groups.

In joint work with Theodore Weisman, we prove generalizations of these classical theorems for the setting of discrete convergence groups. These are groups GG acting on a compact space MM with dynamics mimicking that of Kleinian groups acting on the Riemann sphere ∂ℍℝ3≅ℂ∪{∞}\partial\mathbb H^3_{\mathbb R}\cong\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}. Our dynamical assumptions closely resemble the ping-ping lemma of geometric group theory, and the below picture shows the adapted ping-pong dynamics which arise from our assumptions. These dynamics are analyzed carefully in our proof.

The diagram has a large red disk B1B_1 on the left and a large blue disk B2B_2 on the right, tangent at the point labeled Λ(J)\Lambda(J). Inside B1B_1, several blue circles contain smaller red circles; these red circles contain further blue circles. The pattern is reversed inside B2B_2, where red circles contain smaller blue circles. The nested circles become progressively smaller toward Λ(J)\Lambda(J). A blue circle in the upper left is labeled T1,2T_{1,2}, and a red circle in the upper right is labeled T1,1T_{1,1}.

We end with a possible application of these theorems for studying different types of convergence for sequences of representations which act as convergence groups. Specifically, we study when the algebraic limit and geometric limit of a sequence do not coincide. Such examples exist for Kleinian groups using methods which do not generalize well to other matrix groups, and these combination theorems give a possibility for generalization to other settings.
